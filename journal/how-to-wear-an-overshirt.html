How to Wear an Overshirt

September 28, 2026 · 4 min read

An overshirt is a shirt built with enough structure and room to work as a light jacket. Not a shirt you happen to leave open, and not a jacket with a collar — a specific thing, cut deliberately between the two.

It has become the most useful layer in a lot of wardrobes for a boring reason: it covers the temperature range where a coat is too much and a tee is not enough, which in most climates is about half the year.

How to tell it apart

  • Versus a shirt: heavier fabric, roomier through the body, and cut to sit over another layer without pulling. A shirt worn open over a tee usually looks like a shirt worn open over a tee.
  • Versus a chore coat: a chore coat has more structure, usually four patch pockets, and a squarer shape. An overshirt keeps a shirt collar and shirt cuffs.
  • Versus an overshirt-shaped thing: if it has no cuff placket and the fabric has no body to it, it is a shirt with ambitions.

Four ways to wear one

Open, over a tee. The default, and the reason most people buy one. The tee should be the lighter of the two — the eye wants the brighter thing in the center. A bone tee under an olive overshirt is the version of this that never looks wrong.

Buttoned, as the whole top. Fewer people try this and it is the better-looking option. Button all but the top one or two, leave it untucked, and let the shape do the work. This reads more deliberate than open, which is useful when you want to look like you got dressed on purpose.

Over a hoodie. The hood sits outside the collar, not tucked in. This works when the overshirt is cut generously enough — try it before assuming, because a slim one will pull across the back.

Sleeves rolled, two turns. Not one, which looks accidental, and not four, which looks like you are about to do dishes. Two turns landing just below the elbow.

The most common mistake is treating it as outerwear and sizing up. An overshirt that swamps you stops reading as a garment and starts reading as a mistake.

The fit details that matter

  • Shoulder seam should sit at or just past your shoulder. Past is fine and intentional on a relaxed cut; halfway down your arm is not.
  • Length should cover your waistband and stop around mid-fly. Longer starts to read as a shirt-dress; shorter rides up when you sit.
  • Room across the chest with a tee underneath — you should be able to cross your arms without the placket pulling.

What to pair it with

The easiest combination is a light base and a mid-tone bottom: a bone tee underneath, a stone trouser below, the olive overshirt open over the top. Three pieces, one of which is doing all the visual work, which is what you want.

For colder days the same thing with a grey hoodie in place of the tee covers a surprising amount of autumn.
